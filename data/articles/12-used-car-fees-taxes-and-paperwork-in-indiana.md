The advertised price of a used car is rarely the amount you hand over. Taxes, title and registration, and dealer fees bring the total higher. None of this is unusual, but you should see every line before you agree. This guide explains the pieces so you can ask for a clear, itemized out-the-door price.

A note on accuracy: tax rates, fee amounts and procedures change. The information below describes how the system generally works. Confirm current numbers with the Indiana Bureau of Motor Vehicles (BMV) and the Indiana Department of Revenue before you rely on them.

## The pieces of the real price

- **Vehicle price.** The number on the listing or window sticker.
- **Sales tax.** Indiana charges state sales tax on vehicle purchases, currently 7 percent. It is calculated on the taxable amount of the sale, and a trade-in can reduce that amount in many situations. Ask the dealer how they calculate it for your deal.
- **Title fee.** A charge to put the title in your name.
- **Registration and plates.** Registration costs and annual vehicle excise tax depend on the vehicle and the year. These are paid to the BMV.
- **Dealer documentation fee.** A charge for preparing paperwork. Amounts vary by dealer, so ask for it up front.
- **Optional products.** Extended service contracts, GAP coverage and similar items. These are your choice and should be priced separately.

## Ask for an out-the-door price

The clearest way to compare vehicles and dealers is to ask for the total you will pay, in writing, with every line listed. It should include the vehicle price, tax, title, registration, the document fee and any add-ons you chose. If a number appears later that was not on the sheet, ask why.

## Key documents you will see

1. **Buyers Guide.** A window sticker required on used cars sold by dealers. It states whether the car is sold with a warranty or as is, and who pays for repairs.
2. **Bill of sale or purchase agreement.** Lists the vehicle, the VIN, the price and the terms.
3. **Odometer disclosure.** A statement of the mileage at the time of sale.
4. **Title application.** Paperwork to transfer ownership into your name.
5. **Financing contract.** If you finance, the contract with the APR, term, payment and total cost. See [APR, term and total cost](/blog/understanding-apr-loan-term-and-total-cost/).
6. **Insurance proof.** Needed before you drive away.

## Who handles what

Many dealers prepare the title and registration paperwork for you, while others expect you to visit the BMV. Ask at the start who is responsible, how long it takes and what happens if the plates or title are delayed. Keep copies of everything and note the dates.

## Trade-ins and taxes

A trade-in can reduce the taxable amount of the purchase in many cases, which makes it more valuable than its cash value alone. If you owe money on the trade, the payoff is handled in the deal. Learn more in [how to trade in your car](/blog/how-to-trade-in-your-car/).

## Red flags in the paperwork

- Blank spaces in contracts.
- Numbers that do not match what was discussed.
- Pressure to sign before you have read everything.
- Add-ons included without your clear agreement.
- A title that is not in the dealer's name or has a brand you were not told about.

If you see any of these, pause and ask. Reputable dealers welcome the questions in [15 questions to ask a used car dealer](/blog/questions-to-ask-a-used-car-dealer/).

## Keep the records

Save your bill of sale, odometer statement, financing contract and registration in one folder. You will need them for insurance, repairs, resale and taxes.

## Frequently asked questions

### Is sales tax charged on a used car in Indiana?

Yes. Indiana applies sales tax to vehicle purchases, with some exceptions and rules about trade-ins. Ask the dealer to show the calculation, and check the Department of Revenue for current information.

### Can I refuse a dealer add-on?

Optional products are optional. You should be able to decline them, and the contract should not include them without your agreement.

### How long does it take to get my plates?

It depends on the process the dealer uses and the BMV. Ask for the expected timeline and temporary tags, if available, before you leave.
