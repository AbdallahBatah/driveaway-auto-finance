Nothing slows a car purchase like a missing document. Arriving prepared can turn a long afternoon into a short visit, and it shows the lender you are organized. Requirements vary a little by dealer and lender, so treat this as a strong starting list and call ahead to confirm.

## The essentials

- **Valid driver's license.** It is needed for the test drive and the contract. The name should match your other documents.
- **Proof of income.** Recent pay stubs are the most common proof. If you are self-employed or have other income, bring bank statements, tax returns or benefit letters.
- **Proof of residence.** A recent utility bill, lease or bank statement with your current address.
- **Proof of insurance.** You will need coverage in place to drive the car home. Call your insurer with the VIN so the policy is ready.
- **Down payment.** Know how you will pay it: cash, debit or another method the dealer accepts. Ask in advance about limits on card payments.

## If you have a trade-in

- The **title**, or the lender's information if you still have a loan. See [how to trade in your car](/blog/how-to-trade-in-your-car/).
- A **payoff letter or account details** if there is a loan balance.
- **Registration** and all the **keys and remotes** you have.
- Maintenance records, if you kept them. They help show the car was cared for.

## For financing

Lenders, especially those working with limited credit, may ask for personal references with names and phone numbers, your employer's contact details, and information about previous addresses. Having these ready makes approval faster. For what lenders consider, see [financing with bad credit](/blog/used-car-financing-with-bad-credit/).

## Other things worth bringing

- A **cosigner**, if you are using one, with their ID and proof of income.
- A **list of cars** you have looked at and your payment ceiling. The [inventory](/inventory/) shows stock numbers you can note down.
- A **phone** for photos of the odometer, VIN and window sticker, and for calling your insurer.
- A **notepad** with your questions. Use [15 questions to ask a dealer](/blog/questions-to-ask-a-used-car-dealer/) as a base.
- A **second person** if you want a second opinion.

## Before you go: five quick steps

1. Call ahead and confirm the car you want is still available.
2. Ask which documents the dealer and lender need for your situation.
3. Get your insurance quote or policy change started.
4. Decide your maximum monthly payment and down payment.
5. Plan enough time so you do not feel rushed when you read the contract.

## At the dealership

Ask for copies of everything you sign. Check that the numbers on the contract match what you agreed: price, down payment, APR, term and monthly payment. Do not leave with blanks in the paperwork.

## Plates, registration and title

After the sale, you will need to register the vehicle and get plates through the Indiana BMV. The dealer will explain what they handle and what you do yourself, and the details change over time, so confirm the current process with the BMV. ## Frequently asked questions

### Can I buy a car with only a debit card?

Often a down payment can be made by debit, but limits vary. Ask the dealer ahead of time how they accept payments.

### Do I need a cosigner?

Not always. A cosigner can help if your income or credit is limited, but it also puts the cosigner on the hook for the loan, so it is a serious request.

### What if I forgot something?

Call the dealership. Many documents can be sent by photo or email, and some can wait a short time. Do not sign anything you do not understand because you are in a hurry.
