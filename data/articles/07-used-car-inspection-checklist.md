You do not have to be a mechanic to catch most of the problems that matter. A calm, methodical walkaround, done in daylight and without a salesperson rushing you, will reveal a lot. Print this list or keep it on your phone, and take your time.

## Before you look at the car

Ask for the vehicle history and any service records. Confirm the VIN on the paperwork matches the VIN on the dashboard and the driver's door jamb. Look the car over when the engine is cold, because a warm engine can hide start-up problems.

## Exterior

- **Panels and gaps.** Uneven gaps between doors, hood and trunk can point to past accident repair.
- **Paint.** Look along the side in good light for mismatched color, overspray on rubber trim or a rough texture.
- **Rust.** Northwest Indiana winters mean road salt. Check wheel arches, rocker panels, door bottoms and the underside where you can see it. Surface rust is common; flaking or holes are serious.
- **Glass.** Chips and cracks can spread and may fail inspection or hurt visibility.
- **Lights.** Test headlights, brake lights, signals and hazards.

## Tires and brakes

Tires tell a story. Check the tread depth, look for even wear across the tread and make sure all four tires match in brand and size. Uneven wear can indicate alignment or suspension trouble. Look through the wheels at the brake rotors for deep grooves or heavy rust, and check the fluid reservoir level.

## Under the hood

With the engine off, check the oil on the dipstick. It should be amber to dark brown, not milky or gritty. Look at the coolant level and color, inspect belts and hoses for cracks, and check for stains, drips or fresh-washed spots that might hide a leak. Look at the battery terminals for corrosion.

## Interior

- Press every button: windows, locks, mirrors, seat controls, wipers and the radio.
- Run the air conditioning and the heater at full and at low settings.
- Smell for mildew, smoke or heavy air freshener covering something.
- Check seat belts, upholstery wear and the carpet for dampness, which can signal a leak.
- Compare wear on the pedals and steering wheel with the mileage. Heavy wear on a low-mileage car deserves a question.

## Start-up and warning lights

When you turn the key, all dashboard lights should come on briefly and then go out. A check engine light that stays on, or one that has been disconnected, is a warning sign. Listen for knocking, ticking or squealing on a cold start, and watch the exhaust for heavy smoke.

## Underneath and the paperwork

Look under the car for fresh drips or damage. Verify the title status and ask about open recalls using the VIN on the manufacturer's recall lookup. Our guide to [reading a vehicle history report](/blog/how-to-read-a-vehicle-history-report/) explains what to look for.

## Take it for a drive

A checklist on the lot is only half the job. Follow it with a proper [test drive](/blog/how-to-test-drive-a-used-car/) on roads you know, including a stretch at highway speed.

## Consider an independent inspection

For any car you are serious about, a pre-purchase inspection by a mechanic you trust is inexpensive protection. A reputable dealer will allow it. If a seller refuses, treat that as information.

## When to walk away

Walk away if the seller will not show the title, the VIN does not match, there are signs of frame damage or flood, or you feel rushed. There are plenty of other cars. You can browse the current [inventory](/inventory/) and compare up to three at a time.

## Frequently asked questions

### How long should an inspection take?

A careful walkaround takes about 20 to 30 minutes, plus the test drive. A mechanic's inspection typically adds an hour or so.

### Is a clean history report enough?

No. Reports only show what was reported. Combine it with your own inspection and ideally a mechanic's review.

### What are the biggest red flags?

Frame or structural damage, signs of flooding, a mismatched VIN, a title problem, a disabled check engine light and a seller who will not allow an inspection.
