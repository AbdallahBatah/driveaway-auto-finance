A test drive is your best chance to feel how a car behaves before you own it. Ten minutes around the block is not enough. Plan a route that includes city streets, a rough patch, a hill if there is one, and a few minutes at highway speed. Here is a 12-point plan.

## Before you pull out

1. **Adjust everything.** Set the seat, mirrors and steering wheel. You cannot judge comfort and visibility if you are straining.
2. **Start it cold if you can.** Listen for rough idling, ticking, knocking or squealing, and check that the dashboard lights come on and then go out.
3. **Turn the radio off.** You are listening for the car, not the playlist.

## On city streets

4. **Pull away gently.** The transmission should engage smoothly without a clunk or delay.
5. **Brake firmly in a safe spot.** The car should stop straight, without pulling, vibration through the pedal or grinding.
6. **Feel the steering.** There should be no wandering, play or clunks over bumps. In a straight section, the car should track straight with light hands on the wheel.
7. **Find a rough patch or speed bump.** Listen for rattles, clunks and bounce that suggests worn suspension.

## On the highway

8. **Merge and accelerate.** The engine should pull strongly and the transmission should shift without hesitation or flaring.
9. **Hold 60 to 65 mph.** Watch for vibration in the wheel or seat, wind noise from poor door seals and any drift.
10. **Check the gauges.** The temperature should stay in the normal range and warning lights should stay off.

## After you park

11. **Run the features.** Test the A/C and heat, windows, locks, parking sensors, backup camera and any infotainment features you will use.
12. **Look underneath and under the hood again.** After a drive, fresh drips or smells can show up that were not there before.

## What to pay attention to with different vehicles

For an SUV or truck, check visibility, how it feels turning in a tight parking lot and whether it is comfortable at highway speed. For a sedan, notice road noise and seat comfort. ## Do not rush the decision

A good test drive might leave you wanting a second one on a different day, or a mechanic's inspection. That is normal and wise. If you feel pressure to decide on the spot, treat that as a signal to slow down. Compare the car with one or two others from the [inventory](/inventory/) before choosing.

## Practical details

Bring your driver's license. Dealers usually require it and proof of insurance may be requested. Check your own insurance rules if you drive often, and keep the test drive route varied.

## Frequently asked questions

### How long should a test drive be?

Aim for at least 20 to 30 minutes, with a mix of roads and some highway driving.

### Can I take the car to my mechanic?

Many dealers allow a pre-purchase inspection. Ask before you commit, and ask about any conditions.

### What if I notice a small problem?

Write it down and ask about it. Some issues are cheap to fix, some are not. Negotiate with the cost in mind, or choose another car.
