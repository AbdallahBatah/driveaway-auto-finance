Buying a used car is easier when you do the steps in the right order. Most people get into trouble by starting with the car and leaving the budget for last. This guide walks through the process the way it works best in practice, with notes that apply to buyers in Northwest Indiana.

## Step 1: Decide what the car has to do

Write down the three or four things the vehicle must handle. A commute on I-65 and the Borman is a very different job from hauling kids to practice or towing a small trailer. Be honest about passengers, cargo, winter driving and how many miles you drive a year. This list keeps you from paying for features you will never use.

## Step 2: Set the budget before you shop

Work out the monthly payment you can carry comfortably, then add the costs people forget: insurance, fuel, registration and routine maintenance. Our guide to [how much car you can afford](/blog/how-much-car-can-i-afford/) shows a simple way to do the math. If you plan to finance, talk about the payment you want, not the price you hope to hit. The payment is what you live with every month.

## Step 3: Get the financing conversation started early

Whether you finance through a bank, a credit union or a dealer, knowing your options before you fall for a car gives you leverage and removes pressure. If your credit is limited or damaged, it is worth reading about [what to expect](/blog/used-car-financing-with-bad-credit/) so nothing surprises you. You can also send a [quick application](/financing/) and a real person will call you back.

## Step 4: Search the inventory

Use the online [inventory](/inventory/) to filter by body style, price and brand, and compare up to three vehicles side by side. Look for listings with clear photos, mileage, trim and a stock number. A listing that hides the price or the mileage is a listing to question. Shortlist three to five cars so the next steps stay manageable.

## Step 5: Check the history and the condition

Ask for the vehicle history report and read it properly. Our guide to [reading a history report](/blog/how-to-read-a-vehicle-history-report/) explains what matters. Then walk around the car with our [inspection checklist](/blog/used-car-inspection-checklist/) and take it for a proper [test drive](/blog/how-to-test-drive-a-used-car/).

## Step 6: Ask the right questions

Before you talk numbers, ask about fees, the title, any open recalls and what the dealer will do if something is found after the sale. The list in [questions to ask a used car dealer](/blog/questions-to-ask-a-used-car-dealer/) will keep the conversation honest and short.

## Step 7: Understand the full price

The sticker price is only part of the picture. Indiana charges state sales tax on vehicle purchases, and there are title and registration costs on top of any dealer fees. Rules and amounts change, so confirm current figures with the Indiana BMV and ask the dealer for an itemized, out-the-door price in writing. We cover this in [used car fees, taxes and paperwork in Indiana](/blog/used-car-fees-taxes-and-paperwork-in-indiana/).

## Step 8: Read before you sign

Read every page. Check that the VIN, mileage, price, down payment, APR, term and monthly payment match what you were told. If you are trading in a vehicle, make sure the trade value is stated clearly. If something does not match, stop and ask. A good dealership will not mind.

## Step 9: Arrange insurance and pick up the car

You will need proof of insurance to drive off the lot, so call your insurer with the VIN before you arrive. Bring your documents (see [what to bring](/blog/what-to-bring-to-buy-a-car/)) and set aside time. Before you leave, make sure you have all keys, the paperwork and a plan for the plates and registration.

## Step 10: Keep a simple record

Save your bill of sale, financing contract, title paperwork and inspection notes in one folder. Set a reminder for the first oil change and a check of the tires and brakes. A used car you maintain from day one lasts longer and resells better.

## Frequently asked questions

### Should I buy from a dealer or a private seller?

A dealer usually handles paperwork, offers financing and lets you compare several cars in one place. A private sale can be cheaper but puts the inspection, title transfer and payment risk on you. Neither is automatically better, so choose the one whose risks you are comfortable managing.

### How long does buying a used car take?

Shopping and research take days or weeks. At the dealership, many buyers finish in a couple of hours if their documents are ready and the financing is straightforward. Calling ahead helps the car be ready.

### Can I buy a used car with bad credit?

Yes, many buyers do. Expect to be asked for proof of income and residence, and to talk about a down payment. The article on [bad credit financing](/blog/used-car-financing-with-bad-credit/) goes through it in detail.
