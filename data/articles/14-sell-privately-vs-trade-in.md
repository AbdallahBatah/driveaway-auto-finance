You have two main ways to part with your car: sell it to another person, or trade it in at a dealership. Neither is always better. The right choice depends on how much money you want, how much effort you are willing to spend and how quickly you need to move.

## The case for selling privately

Selling to another person generally brings the highest price, because you are not paying for a dealer's inspection, reconditioning and profit margin. If you have a popular, well-kept vehicle and time to spare, it can be worth the extra effort.

What it takes:

- Cleaning and photographing the car well.
- Writing an honest listing and answering messages.
- Meeting buyers, handling test drives and negotiating.
- Dealing with the title transfer and payment safely.

## The risks of a private sale

- **Scams.** Fake checks, overpayment schemes and pressure to ship or hand over a car before funds clear are common. Accept only payment you can verify, ideally at a bank.
- **Liability.** Complete the title transfer and notify the BMV so you are not responsible after the sale.
- **Time.** A good car can sell quickly, but a vehicle with problems may sit for weeks.
- **Loan complications.** If you owe money, the lender holds the title, which adds steps.

## The case for trading in

A trade-in is fast and low-effort. You bring the car, get an appraisal, and the value goes straight toward your next purchase. In many situations, a trade-in may also lower the sales tax you owe on the next vehicle, which narrows the gap with a private sale. And if you are financing, the dealership can handle the payoff on an existing loan. See [how to trade in your car](/blog/how-to-trade-in-your-car/).

## Side by side

| | Private sale | Trade-in |
|---|---|---|
| Price | Usually higher | Usually lower |
| Time | Days to weeks | Same visit |
| Effort | High | Low |
| Safety risk | You manage it | Handled by business |
| Paperwork | You do it | Dealer helps |
| Tax effect | None | May reduce tax on next car |

## A simple way to decide

1. Get a trade-in estimate. It costs nothing and takes minutes. Start on our [sell or trade page](/sell/).
2. Research what similar cars are listed for in your area.
3. Estimate the real cost of selling privately: your time, ad fees, repairs and the risk of a failed sale.
4. Compare the net numbers. If the gap is small, the convenience of a trade-in usually wins. If it is large, a private sale may be worth the work.

## Special situations

- **You owe more than the car is worth.** A trade-in can include the balance in a new loan, but that raises your cost. Selling privately would require you to pay the difference up front.
- **The car has mechanical problems.** A dealer may still buy it, and the process is easier than convincing a private buyer.
- **You need a vehicle immediately.** A trade-in lets you leave in a car the same day.
- **You want the most cash.** A private sale is the path, if you can handle the work.

## Using the proceeds well

Whichever path you pick, the money is often best used as a [down payment](/blog/down-payment-on-a-used-car/) on the next car. A bigger down payment means a smaller loan, which is the easiest way to keep the payment comfortable. ## Frequently asked questions

### Which gives me more money, selling or trading?

Selling privately usually brings more cash. Trading in is faster and may offer tax advantages, so compare net results.

### Can I sell my car to a dealer without buying one?

Many dealers buy cars outright. Ask whether the business makes cash offers and what documents are needed.
