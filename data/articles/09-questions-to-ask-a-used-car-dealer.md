The right questions save you money and stress. They also tell you a lot about the dealership: honest businesses answer directly and put things in writing. Here are fifteen questions, grouped by topic, that cover the places where buyers most often get surprised.

## About the car

1. **May I see the vehicle history report?** It tells you about title, reported accidents and mileage records. See [how to read it](/blog/how-to-read-a-vehicle-history-report/).
2. **Do you have the title, and is it clean?** The dealer should hold the title or have a clear path to it.
3. **Are there any open recalls?** Recall repairs are generally free, but you should know.
4. **Do you have service records or know the vehicle's past?** Even partial records are valuable.
5. **Can I take it for an extended test drive or have a mechanic inspect it?** A confident seller says yes. Use our [test drive guide](/blog/how-to-test-drive-a-used-car/).

## About the price

6. **What is the out-the-door price?** This includes the vehicle, taxes, title, registration and every fee. Ask for it in writing.
7. **Which fees are required and which are optional?** Documentation fees are common. Optional add-ons should be your choice.
8. **Is the price negotiable?** Some dealers use firm pricing. It is fine to ask, and fine to compare against similar cars.
9. **How are you valuing my trade-in?** Ask for the trade value separately from the price of the new car. See [how to trade in your car](/blog/how-to-trade-in-your-car/).

## About financing

10. **What are the APR, term and total cost?** A monthly payment without those numbers is only part of the story. Read [APR, term and total cost](/blog/understanding-apr-loan-term-and-total-cost/).
11. **Do you finance in-house or through lenders?** If the dealer is the lender, see [what in-house financing means](/blog/what-is-in-house-financing/).
12. **Do you report payments to the credit bureaus?** On-time payments help your credit only when they are reported.
13. **Is there a penalty for paying early?** You should be able to pay down the loan without a fee.

## About after the sale

14. **Does the car come with any warranty, or is it sold as is?** Used cars are often sold as is, and the window sticker (Buyers Guide) must say so. Ask what, if anything, the dealer will do if a problem appears right after you buy.
15. **What is your policy on returns or exchanges?** Many people assume there is a cooling-off period. In general there is no automatic right to cancel a vehicle purchase, so ask what the dealer offers and get it in writing.

## How to use these questions

You do not need to ask all fifteen out loud. Pick the ones that matter for the car and your situation, write the answers down and compare. If an answer is vague, ask for it in writing. Good dealers expect these questions and respect buyers who ask them.

## What good answers sound like

Good answers are specific: a number, a document, a named policy. Be cautious when you hear "don't worry about that," "we'll take care of it later" or "I can't show you that." Walking away is always an option, and there are other cars to look at in the [inventory](/inventory/).

## Frequently asked questions

### Should I ask these questions by phone or in person?

Both work. Asking by phone or email first saves time and gives you answers in writing.

### Is a doc fee normal?

Dealers commonly charge a documentation fee for processing paperwork. Ask the amount up front and include it in the out-the-door price.

### What does "as is" mean?

It means the dealer is not providing a warranty on the vehicle, so repairs after the sale are your responsibility unless another agreement says otherwise. Read the Buyers Guide on the window.
