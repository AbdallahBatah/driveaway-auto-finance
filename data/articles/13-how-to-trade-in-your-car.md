Trading in is the simplest way to turn your current car into credit toward the next one. You skip listing, showing the car to strangers and handling the paperwork on your own. A little preparation can raise the offer you receive and keep the deal clear.

## Step 1: Know what your car is worth

Look up values on a couple of independent valuation sites and compare them with local listings for similar year, mileage and condition. Expect a trade-in offer to sit below what a private buyer might pay, because the dealer has to inspect, recondition and resell the vehicle. Having a realistic range keeps you from being surprised and helps you recognize a fair offer.

## Step 2: Check what you owe

Call your lender and ask for the payoff amount. If your car is worth more than you owe, the difference is equity, and it works like a down payment on the next vehicle. If you owe more than it is worth, that gap is negative equity. It can be rolled into a new loan, but that raises your balance and your cost, so decide carefully. Our [down payment guide](/blog/down-payment-on-a-used-car/) explains why.

## Step 3: Gather the paperwork

- Title, or loan account details if a lender holds it.
- Current registration.
- All keys, remotes and the owner's manual.
- Service records, which show the car was maintained.

## Step 4: Make the car look cared for

A clean car reads as a well-kept car. Remove personal items, vacuum, wash and wipe down the interior. You do not need expensive repairs, but fixing something minor and cheap, such as a burned-out bulb, can help. Do not spend big money on repairs that the dealer can do for less.

## Step 5: Get more than one offer

Ask for offers from more than one source. A dealer's trade-in appraisal is free, and some businesses will buy your car even if you do not buy from them. Our [sell or trade page](/sell/) explains how to start, and you only need the VIN and mileage to begin.

## Step 6: Separate the numbers

When you negotiate, discuss the price of the car you are buying and the value of your trade-in as two separate items. Combining them makes it harder to see whether you are getting a fair deal on either. Ask the dealer to show both figures on the paperwork.

## Step 7: Understand the tax effect

In many cases a trade-in lowers the amount subject to sales tax on the next car, which increases its real value to you. Ask how the dealer handles it and confirm with official sources. See [fees, taxes and paperwork in Indiana](/blog/used-car-fees-taxes-and-paperwork-in-indiana/).

## Step 8: Close the loop on your old car

After the trade, confirm the dealer will pay off your old loan if there is one, and keep proof. Cancel or transfer your insurance for the old car only when the new one is covered, and remove license plates if required.

## What affects your offer

- **Mileage and condition.** Lower mileage and good condition raise the value.
- **Accident history.** Reported damage lowers it.
- **Local demand.** Trucks and SUVs are in steady demand in our area, especially before winter.
- **Mechanical issues.** Known problems will be deducted, so be honest.
- **Color and options.** Smaller factors, but they can matter.

## Frequently asked questions

### Can I trade in a car that is not paid off?

Yes. The dealer pays the lender and applies any equity to your next purchase. Negative equity can be added to a new loan.

### Do I need to fix my car before trading it?

Usually no. Small cosmetic fixes may help, but major repairs rarely pay for themselves in a trade-in.

### Will a dealer take a car with problems?

Often, yes, but the offer will reflect the repair cost. Be upfront about issues so the number is realistic.
